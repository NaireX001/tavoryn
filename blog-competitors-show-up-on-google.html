Why your competitors show up on Google and you don't

TAVORYN TeamNovember 24, 20266 min read

Showing up on Google locally isn't luck, and it isn't only for big companies with big budgets. It comes down to a handful of things Google looks for, and most of them you can fix.

Try this: open Google, type in the service you offer plus your town, "wedding photographer Lagos" or "jewelry appraiser St. Petersburg," and look at what comes up.

If you see three competitors in the map box at the top and your business is nowhere to be found, you're not alone. It's one of the most common frustrations we hear from business owners. You know you're good at what you do, maybe better than the people showing up, but customers can't choose you if they can't find you.

The good news is that showing up on Google locally isn't luck, and it isn't only for big companies with big budgets. It comes down to a handful of things Google looks for, and most of them are things you can fix.

This is called local SEO (search engine optimisation), and here's how it works in plain English.

How Google decides who shows up

When someone searches for a local service, Google shows a map with a short list of businesses, often called the "local pack." These spots get a large share of the clicks and calls, because they appear above everything else.

Google says it chooses who appears there based on three things:

  • Relevance: how well your business matches what the person searched for. If you never clearly say "jewelry appraisal" anywhere, Google can't connect you to that search.
  • Distance: how close you are to the person searching, or to the place they typed in.
  • Prominence: how well known and trusted your business looks online, judged by things like reviews, your website and mentions around the web.

You can't move your shop closer to every customer. But relevance and prominence are almost entirely in your hands, and that's where your competitors are probably beating you.

1. Your Google Business Profile is missing or half-finished

Your Google Business Profile is the free listing that shows your business on Google Maps and in that local box. It's the single most important tool for local search, and many businesses either don't have one or set it up once and forgot about it.

Common signs

  • You've never claimed or verified your profile.
  • The wrong business category is selected, or only one when several apply.
  • There are no photos, or just a blurry logo.
  • Opening hours, phone number or website link are missing or out of date.

How to fix it

  • Claim and verify your profile at google.com/business.
  • Choose the right primary category, the one that best describes your main service, then add secondary categories for the others.
  • Fill in every section: services, service area, hours, description, booking link and website.
  • Add real photos of your work, your space and your team, and keep adding new ones.
  • Post updates about offers, events or new work. An active profile shows Google, and customers, that you're open for business.

2. Your competitors have more (and newer) reviews

Reviews are one of the clearest signals of prominence. When Google, and a customer, sees one business with plenty of recent five-star reviews and another with three reviews from years ago, the choice is easy.

How to fix it

  • Ask every happy client. Most people are glad to leave a review; they just need to be asked at the right moment, usually right after a great result.
  • Make it easy. Send a direct review link by text or email so it takes them seconds.
  • Reply to every review, good and bad. A short, warm reply shows you care, and a calm response to a negative review can win more trust than it loses.
  • Keep them coming. A steady flow of new reviews matters more than a big batch once a year.

3. Your website doesn't say what you do and where

Google reads your website to understand your business. If your homepage says "Welcome! We're passionate about helping you shine," Google has no idea what you sell or where you are.

How to fix it

  • Use the words your customers search for. If people search "estate jewelry appraisal," those words should appear on your site, naturally, in headings and text.
  • Mention your location in the right places: page titles, headings, your footer and your contact page.
  • Give each main service its own page, so Google has a clear page to show for each search.
  • Write clear page titles and descriptions. These are what appear in Google results, so make them say what you do, where, and why someone should click.

4. Your business details don't match across the internet

Your business name, address and phone number, often called your NAP, appear in lots of places: your website, Google, Facebook, Instagram, Yelp and local directories. If they're different in each place, an old address here or a different phone number there, Google becomes less sure which details are correct, and less confident about showing you.

How to fix it

  • Write down your exact business name, address and phone number, and use exactly the same version everywhere.
  • Search for your business name and update any old listings you find.
  • Make sure your details on your website match your Google Business Profile word for word.

5. Your website is slow or hard to use on a phone

Most local searches happen on phones, often when someone needs a service soon. Google favours websites that load quickly and work well on mobile, and customers do too. If your site takes ages to load or is awkward to use on a small screen, people hit the back button and choose the next result.

How to fix it

  • Test your website on your own phone. Does it load quickly? Can you tap the buttons easily?
  • Compress large images before uploading them, as they're one of the biggest causes of slow pages.
  • Put your phone number and booking button where mobile visitors can tap them straight away.

Customers can't choose you if they can't find you.

Your 30-day local SEO plan

You don't have to do everything at once. Here's a simple plan to start closing the gap on your competitors this month.

Week 1: Get your Google Business Profile right

Week 2: Start collecting reviews

Week 3: Fix your website wording

Week 4: Clean up and keep going

Local SEO isn't instant. It usually takes weeks to months to see steady movement. But each step builds on the last, and businesses that stay consistent are the ones that end up in the top spots.

Get found by the customers already looking for you

People in your area are searching for what you offer right now. The only question is whether they find you or your competitors.
